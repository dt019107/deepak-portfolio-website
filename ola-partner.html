01 / CONTEXT & PROBLEM

Who the users are and what was broken

Commercial auto and cab drivers in Mumbai operate in intense, high-stress environments: moving through bumper-to-bumper traffic, managing mounted smartphone mounts under direct afternoon sunlight, and coping with frequent cellular network drops.

Data indicated that 34% of ride dispatches timed out unaccepted within the standard 15-second countdown. Exit surveys revealed that drivers were not ignoring trips intentionally; rather, tiny UI elements, cluttered fare breakdowns, and washed-out color contrasts made it hazardous to evaluate and accept trips while driving.

02 / RESEARCH & EVIDENCE

Field studies with 14 commercial drivers

To understand the actual driving context rather than armchair assumptions, I conducted ride-alongs and on-ground interviews with 14 full-time commercial drivers across Bandra, Andheri, and Dadar transit hubs.

1. Harsh Sunlight Blindness

Standard low-contrast grayscale typography became completely illegible through scratched windshield mounts under midday glare.

"I have to take my hand off the wheel and shield the phone with my palm to read the fare." — Driver Ramesh

2. Decision Fatigue Under 15s

Drivers need only three primary data points before committing: Net Earnings, Pickup Distance, and Drop Neighborhood.

"If I can't calculate my fuel cost in 2 seconds, I let the timer expire." — Driver Santosh

3. False Rejections via Multi-Tap

The legacy confirmation modal required two precise taps (Accept → Confirm), leading to accidental dismissals on bumpy roads.

"Potholes make my finger tap Cancel when I wanted to tap Accept." — Driver Iqbal
03 / DEFINE & GOALS

Problem statement and measurable success metrics

Problem Statement: How might we design a ride dispatch interface that allows drivers to make safe, accurate trip evaluations and accept trips in under 3 seconds under harsh outdoor glare?

Primary Success Metric

Reduce driver dispatch timeout rate and boost 30-day driver platform retention by at least 15%.

Operational Velocity

Cut time-to-decision from an average of 8.2 seconds down to under 5 seconds.

Safety & Usability Benchmark

Achieve 100% single-handed touch accessibility with a minimum 64px tap target height.

04 / IDEATION & DECISION

2 explored directions and why one was chosen

I explored two primary interaction architectures to replace the cumbersome two-tap modal:

Explored & Rejected

Option A: Slide-to-Accept Slider

A horizontal swipe gesture across the bottom screen bar to prevent accidental taps.

Why it failed: During bumpy road tests, drivers failed swipe completions 28% of the time because thumb contact broke mid-drag.

Validated & Shipped

Option B: High-Contrast Full-Width Tap Bar

A massive 72px green touch trigger anchored to the natural thumb zone with clear haptic feedback and distinct red cancel anchor.

Why it won: Delivered a 98% first-try success rate even while driving over rough asphalt, without requiring drivers to stare at the screen.

05 / DESIGN & RATIONALE

Annotated final UI decisions

Rather than superficial visual polish, every layout decision was engineered around sunlight contrast ratios, thumb ergonomics, and cognitive load minimization.

  • 1
    High-Contrast Amber Fare Lockup (AAA Conformance): Guaranteed 7:1 contrast ratio against deep ink backgrounds, allowing drivers to scan net earnings in direct afternoon glare without squinting.
  • 2
    Prominent Pickup & Drop Landmark Badges: Displaying cross-streets and recognizable local landmarks rather than unformatted GPS addresses so drivers instantly recognize routing feasibility.
  • 3
    Circular Countdown Ring with Haptic Pulses: Replaced anxiety-inducing ticking numbers with a calming radial progress perimeter that pulses haptically at 5s remaining.
  • 4
    72px Thumb-Anchored Accept Trigger: Positioned at the bottom third of the display for effortless single-finger tapping on dashboard-mounted smartphones.
06 / USABILITY TESTING

What was tested, what failed, and what changed

I ran two rounds of moderated usability sessions using interactive Figma prototypes running directly on test Android devices mounted on car dashboards in daylight.

The Test

Simulated incoming ride alerts while drivers were asked to navigate dummy map checkpoints.

What Failed

Initial designs placed the cancellation cross icon near the top right. Drivers couldn't reach it safely without leaning forward in their seats.

The Fix

Relocated the Pass/Decline action into a secondary bottom pill separated from the primary Accept trigger by 18px of safety margin.

08 / REFLECTION

What I'd do differently next time

  • Incorporate ambient light sensor triggers: Auto-switching to high-contrast ultra-dark mode when driving into highway tunnels or parking basements.
  • Test audio cues alongside visual feedback: Testing voice-synthesized landmark readouts ("Trip to Dadar Station, 2.5 km") to allow drivers to keep their eyes entirely on the road.
  • Conduct long-tail battery consumption benchmarks: Measuring the power draw of the high-refresh countdown animation across budget Android chipsets during 10-hour driving shifts.
